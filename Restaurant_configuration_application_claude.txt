What they’re really testing

This is for an AI Sales Engineer role, so it’s not just about code. They want to see how you work with AI, how you handle two stakeholders who disagree, and whether you can build something a non-technical customer could use.
The scenario

Harbor Street Kitchen is a restaurant group with 4 locations.
Each store manager needs to see the menu and make changes for their own store.
Changes apply to one location only. If one store raises a burger to $12, the other three keep their own price.
What the app must do

Make it obvious which store is selected.
Show at least 5 menu items, each with a name, category, price, and availability.
Let the manager change an item’s price at that store.
Let the manager mark an item available or unavailable at that store.
Fake sample data is fine. You don’t need a backend, a login, or payments.
The conflict (the real test)

The ops leader wants managers to update every store from one screen so nothing gets delayed.
The IT lead says a manager must never accidentally change another store.
You choose how the app handles this and explain why. Pay attention to the word “accidentally.” It suggests the answer doesn’t have to be all-or-nothing.
What you turn in

The working app, plus short instructions for opening it.
A short explanation of how you handled the conflict.
A full transcript, in order, of every message with every AI tool you used. Screenshots or recordings don’t count.
Rules that are easy to miss

Don’t use any real customer data or company material.
What “good UX, technically sound” means here

It’s hard to make mistakes: the app shows what changed and asks before saving risky edits.
Bad input is handled, like empty or negative prices.
Price and availability are stored separately for each store, not as one shared value.

I’d do it in this order, since your conflict decision shapes the whole design.

1. Decide the conflict first

By default, managers can only edit the store they’ve selected.
For other stores, add a separate “Apply to other stores” step. The manager checks which stores, sees a before/after preview, and confirms.
This gives ops the speed they want and still meets IT’s rule, because nothing changes at another store by accident.
Keep availability store-only, since it’s usually about one kitchen running out of something.
2. Pick a simple stack and one AI tool

React + Vite + TypeScript (you already know it), with data saved in localStorage.
